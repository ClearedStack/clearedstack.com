ClearedStack v3.1 did not start as a single-person sketch. Its design came out of a structured five-seat architecture council—bringing together Kubernetes platform operations, real-time interface design, queue systems, security compliance, and solo-maintainer pragmatism—followed by specialized engineering reviews.

Every major choice on the site today traces back to a concrete trade-off settled in those discussions.

## The five core shifts

| Design question | Early assumption | Final v3.1 decision | Why it won |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Workflow vs. website** | Static essays describing 10 stages | 10 live deterministic worker repos (`cs-01`..`cs-10`) feeding `/guides/command/`, `/guides/apps/`, `/guides/pipeline/`, and `/guides/decide/` | Real-time status and sanitized errors turn the site into a live operational portal instead of passive text. |
| **Database & state** | Separate per-stage databases | One shared PostgreSQL 17 hub (`clearedstack` namespace) + `hub-queue-api` + local worker spools | Workers hold zero DB passwords, handoffs are atomic, and local spools survive hub restarts. |
| **Git, builds, and deploy** | Heavy third-party suites (Forgejo, Argo CD, CasaOS) | Custom clean-room MIT Go microservices (`cs-git`, `cs-registry`, `cs-runner`, `cs-store`) | Drops control-plane RAM to ~640 MiB so the entire 17-pod platform fits on one 4 GiB `k3s` node. |
| **Edge cases & AI** | AI inside workers for messy pages | **100% deterministic everywhere**; unknown layouts fail closed into `needs_subtool` | Zero token cost, identical output on every cluster, and every solved edge case becomes permanent code. |
| **Public access vs. privacy** | Login wall for proposing edits | **No login lock** (`✎ Edit` anywhere behind a 5-layer anti-spam shield) + private `NetworkPolicy` | Anyone can inspect and improve the platform while internal IPs, tokens, and cluster layout stay hidden. |

## Why the council rejected heavy third-party suites

Bundling Forgejo, Harbor, Argo CD, and CasaOS would have required 8–16 GiB of RAM just to run the control plane, along with dozens of upstream database tables and mixed licenses. By writing four small, single-purpose Go services from scratch under the **MIT License**, ClearedStack keeps the control plane under 640 MiB, removes Docker completely, and avoids copying any upstream code.

## Why two namespaces beat eleven

An early draft proposed 11 separate namespaces (one for the hub and one per worker). The council simplified that to **two namespaces**: `clearedstack` for the 7 control-plane pods and `cs-workers` for all 10 deterministic workers (`cs-01-intake` through `cs-10-notify`). Two namespaces keep `NetworkPolicy` isolation clean without multiplying boilerplate manifests for solo maintainers adopting the stack.
