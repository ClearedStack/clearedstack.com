ClearedStack is a catalog of small tools for government contracting. Each tool does one job, such as building a labor rate, pulling the requirements out of a solicitation or marking a document, and each one runs in your browser. There is no account to create, nothing to install and nothing to pay.

This guide walks through the site from the top. It takes a few minutes and you will not need it twice.

## Who it is for

The tools are written for people who do the contracting paperwork themselves:

- a solo developer or consultant pricing a first bid
- a founder of a small company answering a first solicitation
- an engineer who has been handed “the compliance part”
- anyone at a larger company who is tired of rebuilding the same spreadsheet

You do not need a background in contracting. The [field guides](/guides/#field) explain the ideas each group of tools is built on, and the [glossary](/guides/glossary/) covers the vocabulary.

## What you need

A current web browser: Firefox, Chrome, Edge or Safari. That is all.

- **No account.** There is no sign-up, no sign-in and no email address to give.
- **No install.** A tool is a web page. Nothing is added to your computer.
- **No payment.** Not now and not later. The project takes in no revenue of any kind.

If your company restricts which websites you can reach, or you work on a network with no internet connection, the whole site can be copied and run inside your own network. See [Host your own copy](/guides/#host).

## Find a tool

Open the [Tools](/tools/) page. The tools are grouped into five areas. Each area has a line-item number, in the style of a contract’s price schedule:

| CLIN | Area | What the tools there do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0001 | Pricing & cost accounting | Build labor rates and indirect rates, lay out a price table, work out interest on a late payment |
| 0002 | Proposal & capture | Take a solicitation apart into requirements and check that your response answers every one |
| 0003 | Cybersecurity & compliance | Work through NIST SP 800-171 and CMMC, mark CUI, look up STIG rules, validate OSCAL files |
| 0004 | Market research & small business | Find the right codes, set-asides and eligibility rules |
| 0005 | Tactical & DevSecOps | Inspect manifests, certificates and file transfers on a network with no internet |

There are three ways to narrow the list:

1. **Type in the filter box.** It searches tool names, descriptions and area names at once, so “rate”, “CUI” or “proposal” all work. Several words narrow the list further: “labor rate” finds tools that mention both.
2. **Use the area buttons** under the filter box to jump to one area.
3. **Follow a link from the home page.** Each line of the price table there opens the matching area.

## Read the status label

Every tool carries one of three labels:

| Label | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| Planned | On the list. Work has not started. |
| In development | Being built. Not usable yet. |
| Live | Ready to use. Its name is a link. |

Right now every tool is marked Planned. The catalog is published first so that people can say which tools they need most, and each tool changes to Live on the day it works. Nothing is marked Live early.

If a tool you need is still Planned, tell the project. Requests decide the build order. [Request a tool or add your own](/guides/request-or-add-a-tool/) explains how.

## Open a tool

When a tool is live, two links appear beside it in the catalog:

- **Its name** opens the tool itself.
- **Source** opens the repository that holds its code.

Each tool is a separate small project with its own repository. That is deliberate. A tool that does one job is small enough for one person to read in a sitting, and it can be copied, changed or hosted without taking the rest of the catalog along.

## What every listed tool has in common

A tool is only added to the catalog if it meets five conditions. They are the same for tools the project builds and tools other people contribute.

1. **Free and open source.** No paid tier, no ads, no “contact sales”.
2. **Client-side.** What you enter is processed on your computer and is not sent to a server.
3. **No tracking.** No analytics, no third-party scripts, no accounts.
4. **Public sources only.** It is built from regulations, standards and guidance the government has officially released, and it says which ones.
5. **Honest about limits.** It says what it does not cover, and that its output is not professional advice.

Conditions 2 and 4 are the ones worth checking for yourself, and both can be checked. The next two guides show how.

## What ClearedStack is not

- **Not a government site.** It is an independent personal project with no affiliation to the Department of Defense or any agency.
- **Not a clearance or a certification.** The name is only a name. Using a tool here does not make a company compliant with anything.
- **Not advice.** A tool gives you a worked answer to check. It does not replace an accountant, a lawyer or a contracting officer’s answer to a question.
- **Not a place for classified information.** No tool listed here is approved for it.

The [About](/about/#disclaimer) page has the full disclaimer.

## Keep up with changes

There is no newsletter and no feed to subscribe to, because the site collects no addresses. To hear when a tool ships, watch the project on [GitHub](https://github.com/ClearedStack). Each tool’s repository has its own releases and its own issue list.
