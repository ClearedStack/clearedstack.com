This website, its guides, and its diagrams are drafted using artificial intelligence tools—specifically Claude Code by Anthropic and Antigravity by Google. Every line of code, prose, and regulatory reference is reviewed, verified, and merged by a single human project owner, who decides what gets built and remains solely accountable for everything on this site.

We do not make claims about how external AI vendors handle or store data behind the scenes. Instead, our policy is based on what this project does: we never put sensitive, proprietary, or non-public information into an AI tool in the first place.

## The division of labor

AI assistants do not set project policy, choose which tools to build, or publish updates autonomously. They serve as authoring and research aids under human direction.

- **What AI tools do:** Draft initial TypeScript components, style layouts, format Markdown and MDX guides, structure complex regulatory ideas into clear steps, build accessible diagram data structures, and draft test cases for mathematical formulas. In the future Prior Art engine, automated sweeps will assist in discovering candidate tools and drafting initial score recommendations.
- **What the human owner does:** Decides what to build and what to decline, directs every architecture decision, independently validates every citation against primary government publications, verifies mathematical formulas with worked manual calculations, and holds sole legal and editorial responsibility.

## The review rule: zero unverified citations

Large language models can produce convincing statements that are entirely incorrect. In federal contracting, an invented FAR clause or an outdated dollar threshold can lead to non-compliant proposals or audit findings.

To prevent this, the project enforces a strict human review rule:

The tools listed in the ClearedStack catalog are not AI wrappers. They contain no large language models, query no third-party machine learning APIs, and make zero network calls. When you open a tool, your browser executes plain, deterministic JavaScript or WebAssembly entirely on your machine. The site’s Content Security Policy (`connect-src 'self'`) guarantees that what you type cannot be transmitted to an AI provider or external server.

## Known failure modes of AI tools

We monitor AI-generated drafts for three common failure modes:

1. **Invented citations.** Language models frequently hallucinate clause numbers that follow realistic FAR or DFARS numbering conventions but do not exist in the published regulation.
2. **Outdated regulatory text.** Models often reference repealed provisions, former statutory dollar thresholds (such as superseded micro-purchase limits), or older revisions of NIST publications.
3. **Confident wrong arithmetic.** Models can articulate calculation steps convincingly while making subtle rounding errors or failing on boundary conditions such as leap years or zero-dollar overhead pools.

The human-in-the-loop review protocol and manual worked examples exist specifically to intercept these errors before anything ships.

## Commit-trailer policy

To ensure transparency across version control history, commits created with the assistance of AI tools carry a trailer naming the tool. Going forward, commit messages adhere to this convention:

```text
Add Prior Art engine documentation

Assisted-by: Claude Code
Assisted-by: Antigravity
```

In addition, entries in the public decision log explicitly identify which AI tool drafted the initial evaluation and which human reviewer signed off on the outcome.

## How to report a mistake

If you find an incorrect statutory citation, a misquoted rule, or a mathematical error in any guide or tool on this site, please let us know immediately:

- **Open a GitHub issue:** Submit a report on [this site’s repository](https://github.com/ClearedStack/clearedstack.com/issues).
- **Send an email:** Write to [contact@clearedstack.com](mailto:contact@clearedstack.com) if you do not use GitHub.

When reporting a mistake, please include the specific page URL, the sentence or calculation in question, what the correct value should be, and the governing regulation or official publication that supports the correction. We verify and correct reported errors promptly.
