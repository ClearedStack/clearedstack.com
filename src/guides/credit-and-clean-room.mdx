Federal procurement rules and public regulations belong to everyone, but software implementations belong to their authors. ClearedStack honors that line through a strict clean-room doctrine: we observe what jobs need doing from public surfaces, but we never copy anyone else’s code, text, design, or data.

When a tool is inspired by an existing product, we say so plainly. We credit the source, explain what we did differently, and give creators an easy, no-questions-asked way to opt out of nominative attribution.

## Build, link, or skip?

ClearedStack does not exist to replace good open-source work. If someone has already built a free, privacy-respecting tool that runs client-side and cites public sources, rebuilding it wastes effort.

The project follows a firm “link over rebuild” preference:

## What we take and what we never take

Every software product consists of functional ideas and copyrightable expression. We draw an absolute boundary between the two.

We observe the job to be done: the public calculation, the statutory threshold, or the regulatory requirement that contractors must meet. We never take the expression that another developer or vendor created to do that job.

When reusable open-source code with a permissive license (such as MIT, BSD, or Apache 2.0) is incorporated into a tool, we preserve all original copyright notices, include the upstream license file, and state the reuse openly in the repository. Strong copyleft code is not mixed into MIT-licensed repositories; compliant copyleft tools are linked externally instead.

## The clean-room build path

To prevent accidental intellectual property contamination, development follows an isolated clean-room procedure. Builders work directly from the governing public regulation and an abstract decision record, never by inspecting the competitor’s implementation.

During observation, researchers observe strict ethical boundaries:
- We read only publicly accessible web pages, product documentation, and public repositories.
- We never register for accounts, start trials, sign non-disclosure agreements, or bypass paywalls.
- We never bypass anti-bot protections, ignore robots.txt directives, or probe undocumented endpoints.
- We never input non-public, proprietary, or Controlled Unclassified Information (CUI) into any external system.

If a commercial tool produces an answer that conflicts with an official government publication, ClearedStack always follows the official publication. The government text is the sole source of truth.

## The provenance note template

Transparency means acknowledging where an idea came from without claiming endorsement or making disparaging comparisons. Every tool inspired by an existing product carries a standardized provenance note in its catalog entry and repository README:

```text
Idea seen in: Tool A (Vendor V), observed YYYY-MM from its public pages.
What we took: the job — [one sentence describing the functional task].
What we did differently: runs entirely in your browser, free, no account; built from [Regulation R].
```

Provenance statements are strictly factual:
- They state that a workflow was observed on a public web page at a specific date.
- They do not rate, criticize, or disparage the observed commercial product.
- They do not imply partnership, sponsorship, or endorsement by the observed vendor.
- Third-party product names are used solely in a nominative sense to identify prior art.

## Handling correction and opt-out requests

We respect creators and tool authors. If a vendor, maintainer, or author contacts ClearedStack regarding an entry, we handle requests through an open, predictable process:

- **Factual corrections.** If an observation record misstates what a tool does, what public rule it implements, or its license terms, we correct the record upon verification.
- **Nominative opt-outs.** If a product owner prefers not to have their product named on ClearedStack, we honor that preference. We replace the product and company name in the provenance note with a neutral phrase (such as “a commercial product”), while maintaining the factual record that an existing tool inspired the workflow.
- **Public source integrity.** Citations to official government statutes, regulations, and standards are never removed, as they belong to the public domain.
